The multi-response model estimates peak particle velocity (PPV) and peak particle acceleration (PPA) for each condition defined by source-to-receptor distance $d$ and maximum instantaneous charge (MIC) $q$. Each response family comprises the longitudinal, transverse, and vertical components and their resultant. Component PPV predictions set the amplitudes of the scenario time histories; PPA predictions complete the demand characterization and provide information for the joint refinement of the responses.

The model-fitting sample combines local IMPALA observations with observations from the supplementary S11D source outside the local distance--MIC envelope, under the hypothesis that their amplitude--distance--charge relations are transferable. Each response uses only observations for which that measurement is available. The covariates include $d$, $q$, scaled distance $\mathrm{SD}=d/\sqrt q$, and their transformations, and the amplitudes are modeled on the decimal logarithmic scale.

Five base learners—linear regression, spline-based generalized additive models, multivariate adaptive regression splines, gradient boosting, and random forests—are evaluated by five-fold cross-validation [@friedman1991; @friedman2001; @breiman2001; @kohavi1995]. For each response, their out-of-fold (OOF) predictions—each observation predicted by a fit that excluded it—are combined using weights inversely proportional to OOF RMSE. The multi-response refinement starts from the independent $t=0$ formulation, which uses only the blasting covariates, and applies Jacobi iterations: a simultaneous update in which all responses at iteration $t$ incorporate the OOF predictions of the companion responses fixed at $t-1$. The iteration with the highest OOF $R^2$ is retained separately for each response. This design preserves the separation between model fitting and evaluation by assessing both the ensemble and its refinement on observations not used to produce their own predictions.

Empirical quantiles of the retained formulation's OOF residuals define the $p50$, $p90$, and $p95$ predictive levels. The longitudinal, transverse, and vertical PPV targets feed scenario construction, whereas PPA characterizes acceleration demand. Improvement over $t=0$ is quantified on the same observation--prediction OOF pairs; it is not inferred from architectural complexity.
