A dynamic-demand scenario comprises the set of three-component realizations associated with a source-to-receptor distance, maximum instantaneous charge (MIC), and predictive level. Each realization combines a source time history processed in L/T/V with the longitudinal, transverse, and vertical PPV targets estimated for that condition, and provides acceleration, velocity, and displacement time histories in the analysis directions H1/H2/UP for the dynamic analysis.

The PPV targets are obtained from the physical predictions of the multi-response model within its domain of applicability. Each L/T/V component is scaled independently to reach its target; the same factor is applied to the acceleration, velocity, and displacement of that component. The scaled horizontal pair is then transformed to the principal directions: H1 is the horizontal direction of maximum variance of the acceleration, H2 its orthogonal, and the scaled vertical component is identified as UP. Because the factors are computed independently for each component, the amplitude ratios among components may differ from those observed in the source time history. This construction preserves the kinematic consistency of each component and incorporates the temporal variability of the instrumental time histories into the scenarios.

The resulting family comprises the H1/H2/UP time histories, the intensity measures—peak amplitudes, Arias intensity, cumulative absolute velocity, and significant durations—and the 5%-damped pseudoacceleration response spectra for each realization. The identity of the source time history and the blasting condition remain associated with these time histories, intensity measures, and response spectra, allowing the subsequent geotechnical response to be related to the applied excitation.
