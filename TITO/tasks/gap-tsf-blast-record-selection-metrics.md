# Gap: Blast Record Selection Metrics for TSF Scenario Analysis

Context:

We are preparing an English technical report for project `AR-S2J2J.R`, which
selects and scales measured blasting records for TSF scenario analysis. The
purpose of record selection is to represent dynamic demand relevant to saturated
fine tailings, pore-pressure accumulation, cyclic mobility, or liquefaction
potential. The research gap is not generic definitions of PPV, PPA, or response
spectra; it is which record descriptors are physically defensible for this
mechanism.

Questions:

1. Which intensity measures or record descriptors are supported in geotechnical
   dynamic or blast-vibration literature for evaluating pore-pressure
   accumulation or liquefaction-type response: component peak acceleration,
   component peak velocity, duration, significant cycles, dominant frequency,
   bandwidth, Arias intensity, cumulative absolute velocity, RMS measures,
   response spectra, Fourier spectra, or other metrics?
2. What formulas define the relevant metrics, and which assumptions or units
   matter when applying them to measured blast acceleration or velocity
   histories?
3. How should frequency content and duration be represented when selecting
   records for saturated fine tailings, where cumulative pore-pressure
   generation may depend on cycles and drainage rather than only peak amplitude?
4. Is there literature support for combining peak component demand with
   cumulative or frequency-content descriptors when selecting records for
   scenario analysis?
5. Which descriptors are weakly supported or potentially misleading for
   blast-induced TSF response, and what limitations should be stated?
