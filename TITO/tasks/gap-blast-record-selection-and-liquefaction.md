# Gap: Blast Records, Frequency Content, and Dynamic Liquefaction Potential

Context:

We are preparing an English technical report for project `AR-S2J2J.R`, which
evaluates the effects of nearby blasting on a tailings storage facility (TSF).
The project processes measured blast vibration histories and selects/scales
scenario records for different blasting scenarios. The research gap is not
generic earthquake record selection and not numerical-software input
requirements. The gap is how blast vibration characteristics, especially
frequency content, duration, number of cycles, component amplitude, and
near-source record scaling, relate to saturated fine tailings, pore-pressure
accumulation, cyclic mobility, and dynamic liquefaction potential.

Questions:

1. What literature connects blast-induced vibration or blast loading to dynamic
   response, pore-pressure generation, cyclic mobility, or liquefaction in
   saturated sands, silts, fine tailings, or mine tailings deposits?
2. How do frequency content, duration, significant cycles, strain amplitude, and
   drainage/permeability conditions influence pore-pressure accumulation in
   saturated fine-grained or tailings-like materials under transient dynamic
   loading?
3. What record-selection criteria are defensible when selecting measured blast
   records for TSF scenario analysis: component peak acceleration, component
   velocity, Arias or cumulative intensity, duration, dominant frequency,
   spectral content, cycle count, distance/charge similarity, or other
   descriptors?
4. How should scaling measured blast records to scenario amplitudes be described
   when the purpose is to evaluate TSF dynamic response and possible pore
   pressure accumulation, without implying earthquake spectral matching or a
   physics-based blast source simulation?
5. What limitations should be stated when using surface or near-field blast
   vibration records as scenario inputs for saturated tailings response,
   especially regarding frequency attenuation, phase relationships, spatial
   variability, drainage assumptions, and uncertainty in translating measured
   acceleration histories into pore-pressure response?
