# Gap: Blasting, Pore Pressure, and Liquefaction in Saturated Tailings

Context:

We are preparing an English technical report for project `AR-S2J2J.R`, which
evaluates nearby blasting effects on a tailings storage facility (TSF). The
engineering concern is saturated fine tailings and possible dynamic pore
pressure accumulation, cyclic mobility, or liquefaction-type response under
transient blast-induced vibration. The project already has measured blasting
records and scenario records; the research gap is the physical and mathematical
basis for relating those dynamic histories to saturated-tailings response.

Questions:

1. Which peer-reviewed papers, technical manuals, or authoritative tailings/dam
   references connect blast-induced vibration or short-duration transient
   dynamic loading to excess pore-pressure generation, cyclic mobility, or
   liquefaction in saturated sands, silts, mine tailings, or fine tailings?
2. What model families or equations are used to relate cyclic stress or cyclic
   strain demand, number of equivalent cycles, drainage conditions, and material
   state to excess pore-pressure ratio or liquefaction triggering?
3. How do the cited methods treat loading frequency, duration, and short
   high-frequency pulses, and what limits are stated when earthquake-derived
   cyclic-liquefaction concepts are applied to blast-like loading?
4. Which material parameters are required to evaluate pore-pressure generation
   or cyclic resistance in saturated tailings, and which of those parameters
   cannot be inferred from acceleration or velocity records alone?
5. What conservative wording should be used when a report evaluates
   blast-record scenarios as dynamic demand indicators rather than as a
   calibrated pore-pressure or liquefaction prediction model?
