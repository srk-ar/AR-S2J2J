# Gap: Scaling Measured Blast Records for TSF Scenario Demand

Context:

We are preparing an English technical report for project `AR-S2J2J.R`, which
uses measured blast vibration records and scales them to scenario amplitudes.
The objective is to construct scenario records for TSF dynamic response review,
not to perform spectral matching, not to synthesize a physics-based blast
source, and not to claim direct pore-pressure prediction from acceleration
records alone.

Questions:

1. In blasting, geotechnical earthquake engineering, or transient dynamic
   analysis literature, what support exists for amplitude-scaling measured
   records for scenario studies?
2. Which properties are preserved by simple amplitude scaling, and which are
   not preserved: frequency content, duration, phase relationships, component
   coherency, wave propagation effects, source/path/site terms, cycle count, or
   pore-pressure-relevant cumulative demand?
3. When measured records are scaled independently by component peaks, what
   assumptions and limitations should be stated for TSF dynamic response
   assessment?
4. How does amplitude scaling differ from spectral matching, target-spectrum
   adjustment, synthetic waveform generation, or calibrated blast source
   modelling?
5. What wording is defensible for describing scaled measured blast records as
   scenario demand histories for saturated-tailings response?
