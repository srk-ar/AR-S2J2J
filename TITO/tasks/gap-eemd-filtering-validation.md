# Gap: EEMD Filtering and Validation for Engineering Vibration Records

Context:

We are preparing an English technical report for project `AR-S2J2J.R`, which
evaluates nearby-blasting effects on a TSF. The audited project workflow uses
EEMD-based decomposition and reconstruction of blast vibration acceleration
histories before scenario products are used for engineering review. The
research gap is not the basic definition of EMD or EEMD. The gap is how much
can be claimed, what limitations must be stated, and which validation checks
are expected when EEMD is used as an engineering filtering/reconstruction method
for transient blast records.

Questions:

1. Which EMD/EEMD filtering references are most authoritative for engineering
   vibration or transient signal records?
2. What does the literature support regarding removal of high-frequency IMFs,
   residual/trend terms, or selected modal components?
3. What known limitations should be reported: mode mixing, end effects,
   non-uniqueness, sensitivity to ensemble/noise parameters, or loss of physical
   interpretability?
4. Which validation checks are recommended or defensible for EEMD-filtered
   engineering histories: peak preservation, energy preservation, frequency
   content, drift/baseline checks, response spectra, visual review, or other
   diagnostics?
5. How should EEMD filtering be described without overclaiming that the retained
   modes are physically unique blast-wave components?
