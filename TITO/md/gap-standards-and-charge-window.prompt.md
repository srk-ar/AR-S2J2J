## CONTEXT

This prompt supports an English technical report for project `AR-S2J2J.R`, which evaluates the effects of nearby blasting on a tailings storage facility (TSF) in a South African mining setting. The research gap concerns which standards, guidance documents, and charge-window definitions are authoritative enough to support professional report wording for a TSF/geotechnical blasting assessment without inventing a compliance criterion. It does not concern basic definitions of PPV, PPA, component peaks, or scaled distance.

## SLOTS

### SLOT 1: Relevant Standards and Guidance Documents

Determine which South African and international standards or guidance documents are most relevant for blast-induced ground-vibration monitoring and reporting in a mining or TSF context. Write in flowing paragraphs.

### SLOT 2: Criteria and Reporting Distinctions

Describe how those sources distinguish geotechnical/TSF effects, embankment or dam-related performance concerns, structural damage references, and monitoring/reporting requirements. Write in flowing paragraphs.

### SLOT 3: Charge Per Delay and Charge Window Definitions

Explain how authoritative blasting references define maximum charge per delay, maximum instantaneous charge, and short-delay charge windows. Write in flowing paragraphs.

### SLOT 4: Status of a 9 ms Charge Window

Assess whether a 9 ms charge window is supported by standards or literature, or whether it should be treated as a project/vendor-specific definition unless project documents say otherwise. Write in flowing paragraphs.

### SLOT 5: Dual-Blast or Multi-Design Events

Describe how dual-blast or multi-design events should be described when charge and distance must be paired by blast design. Write in flowing paragraphs.

## CONSTRAINTS

- Write for an English technical report.
- Focus on the authority of standards, guidance documents, and charge-window definitions rather than basic definitions of PPV, PPA, component peaks, or scaled distance.
- Do not invent a compliance criterion.
