## Background

Production blasting at the mining operations adjacent to the BRPM tailings storage facility (TSF; Impala, Northern Concentrator) generates transient dynamic actions in the saturated fine tailings. This report develops an integrated model of that demand for conditions defined by source-to-receptor distance and maximum instantaneous charge (MIC). The model relates the instrumental records to estimated peak particle velocity (PPV) and peak particle acceleration (PPA) and represents each evaluated condition by a three-component set of acceleration, velocity, and displacement time histories intended for the dynamic deformation model and for the analysis of blast-induced dynamic liquefaction in the critical section of the TSF.

The dynamic response depends on the complete excitation history: duration, cycle sequence, and frequency content act together with peak amplitude. Accordingly, the demand is represented by sets of time-history realizations. The histories in each set share the amplitude targets associated with the evaluated distance and charge and incorporate the temporal variability of the instrumental database.

The quantitative scope of the report concludes with the construction and characterization of these demand histories. The geotechnical response, performance criteria, and reliability conditional on each scenario require the plane-strain effective-stress dynamic model and the geotechnical capacity model, which are currently pending. The final chapter formulates the connection between those future results and the reliability assessment; the corresponding estimates will be incorporated once results from both models become available.

## Objectives

The objective of the study is to provide a robust prediction model of blast-induced seismic demand, expressed as peak particle velocity and acceleration values (PPV and PPA), and to calibrate a suite of blast vibration records for the dynamic deformation model, intended for the analysis of blast-induced dynamic liquefaction. To this end, the study:

- **Characterizes the instrumental data and amplitude observations.** It transforms the measured velocity histories into coherent three-component sets of acceleration, velocity, and displacement time histories, characterizes the temporal and spectral diversity of the population, and documents the available distance and charge metadata.

- **Estimates demand amplitudes.** It obtains PPV and PPA for the longitudinal, transverse, and vertical components and their resultants using a multi-response predictive model. Predictive performance is evaluated with out-of-fold (OOF) evidence, and predictions are expressed through empirical levels over the distance-and-charge domain; S11D observations supplement the model-fitting sample only outside the local envelope, under the transfer assumption documented in the predictive-model chapter.

- **Calibrates the three-component suite of demand records.** It combines the PPV targets from the predictive model with the processed instrumental histories, transforms the horizontal components into the principal horizontal (H1) and orthogonal horizontal (H2) directions, and retains the vertical direction as UP. Each scenario comprises acceleration, velocity, and displacement realizations with their intensity measures and spectra, linked to the source history and the condition that determines their amplitude; the scenario family constitutes the calibrated suite of records delivered to the dynamic deformation model.

- **Formulates the future geotechnical reliability assessment.** It links the pending responses from the dynamic deformation model—including the evaluation of blast-induced dynamic liquefaction—to geotechnical demands, limit states, probabilities of failure, and reliability indices conditional on each scenario. This formulation establishes the proposed method; its results will be obtained from the dynamic model and the geotechnical capacity model.
