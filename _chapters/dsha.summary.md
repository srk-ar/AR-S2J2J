The deterministic assessment postulates, for each tectonic source capable of controlling the site demand, its maximum credible earthquake (MCE): the largest reasonably conceivable earthquake along a recognized fault or within a geographically defined tectonic province under the presently known tectonic framework [@ICOLD2016]. The assessment evaluates the conditional exceedance probability of the ground-motion model, conditioned on the occurrence of the postulated scenario $s = (m_s, \mathbf r_s, \boldsymbol\theta_s)$; the probabilistic assessment integrates that same probability against the recurrence of the sources:

$$
G_s(i^*) \;=\; P\left[I > i^* \mid m_s, \mathbf r_s, \boldsymbol\theta_s\right] \;=\; \sum_{k} w_k \left[1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln i^* - \mu_k}{\sigma_k}\right)\right]
$$

where the components $k=(r,g)$ run over the rupture realizations $r$ of the scenario and the branches $g$ of the logic tree of its tectonic regime, with weights $w_k=v_g/N_R$, in which $v_g$ is the branch weight and $N_R$ the number of realizations; $\mu_k$ and $\sigma_k$ are the mean and the standard deviation of the logarithm of the intensity $I$ for component $k$, $i^*$ is the level evaluated, and $\Phi$ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. The vector $\boldsymbol\theta_s$ collects the rupture geometry -- hypocentral depth, dip, and the style and orientation of the plane, represented by explicit equally weighted variants -- and the site condition of the ground-motion model, fixed at the reference rock ($V_{S30} = 760$ m/s).

The scenario distribution is the complement $F = 1 - G_s$, a finite mixture of lognormal components whose fractiles follow by numerical inversion and whose mean follows in closed form; the evaluation is carried out analytically from the component parameters $(\mu_k,\sigma_k,w_k)$. The reported spectrum for each scenario consists of the median, the 84th, 90th, and 95th percentiles, and the mean of that distribution. The dispersion of the mixture separates its sources: the epistemic component arises from the dispersion of the medians across realizations and models; the aleatory component, from the standard deviation published by each formulation. The comparison in this chapter presents the mean and the 84th percentile of each scenario.

The ordinates correspond to the maximum horizontal component of $S_a(T_n)$ at 5% of critical damping, evaluated at the reference condition $V_{\mathrm{ref}}=760$ m/s; the PGA corresponds to $T_n=0$. That condition constitutes the computational reference from which the site-response model derives the evaluated site conditions.

Each tectonic source is reported as a separate conditional spectrum, preserving the physical identity of the governing earthquake in each spectral band. The deterministic design spectrum is the per-period envelope of the scenario spectra: the reporting nomenclature defines the MCE as the per-period maximum of the scenario means and the MCE (84%) as the maximum of their 84th percentiles. The scenario spectra are compared with the mean uniform hazard spectra of the probabilistic assessment.
