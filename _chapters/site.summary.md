The design of the works is verified against the seismic demand for the site condition of the project location, while the probabilistic assessment resolves the hazard at reference rock. The calculation transfers that hazard to the $V_{S30}$ conditions defined in the seismic site classification and establishes the spectra and accelerations adopted in design. The transfer represents the modification that near-surface geology introduces in the amplitude and in the spectral content of the motion: the linear response according to the stiffness contrast that $V_{S30}$ summarizes, and the nonlinear response according to the input intensity, represented by its PGA. Lower-stiffness profiles may amplify the demand at intermediate-to-long periods and deamplify it at short periods; stiff rock produces minor modifications [@Baker2021; @Stewart2020; @Hashash2020].

The assessment computes two independent branches. The GMPE-based branch evaluates the ground-motion logic tree in OpenQuake at each project $V_{S30}$ condition and preserves the source and ground-motion characterization of the reference-rock assessment: each model contributes its own empirical site term evaluated at the target condition, the logic-tree weights represent the epistemic alternatives adopted, and aleatory variability is integrated in the exceedance curves.

The amplification-based branch starts from the mean uniform hazard spectrum (UHS) at the reference condition $V_{\mathrm{ref}}=760$ m/s and applies to it the ergodic site amplification model of Stewart *et al.* [-@Stewart2020] and Hashash *et al.* [-@Hashash2020]. The factor $F(T_n,V_{S30},pga^*)$ is treated as a lognormal random variable: its mean composes the adjustment between the 760 and 3000 m/s references, the linear site term dependent on $V_{S30}$, and the nonlinear term dependent on the input PGA $pga^*$; its dispersion gathers the contributions of the linear and the nonlinear terms across period, velocity, and intensity. The transformed ordinate is $S_a^{F}=F\,S_a(V_{\mathrm{ref}})$.

For each structural period $T_n$, return period $T_R$, statistic $p$, and $V_{S30}$ condition, the adopted demand is the greater ordinate of the two branches,

$$
S_a^{\max}\!\left(T_n,V_{S30},T_R,p\right)=\max\!\left\{S_a^{\mathrm{GMPE}},\;S_a^{F}\right\},
$$

where $S_a^{\mathrm{GMPE}}$ is the ordinate of the GMPE-based branch and $S_a^{F}$ that of the amplification-based branch. This envelope is the adopted aggregation rule for the design demand: the UHS present the two branches and the PGA and PSA tables adopt the envelope row by row.

The transformation is evaluated period by period, conditional on the PGA of the same realization, which anchors the nonlinear term; the conditional variance preserves the within-event correlation between the spectral ordinate and that PGA. For deterministic scenarios, the calculation propagates the analytic mixture of lognormal components through the amplification factor and integrates over the PGA of each component, and the quantiles of the resulting distribution define the site-condition fractiles; for probabilistic calculations, the dispersion of the amplification model generates the statistics of the transformed demand. Operations on jointly distributed ordinates at several periods—among them the displacement estimation—represent the correlation between periods with the model of Baker and Jayaram [-@BakerJayaram2008]; the spectra are per-period marginal quantities.

The site terms of both branches represent ergodic responses averaged over sets of strong-motion recordings; the characterization of a specific stratigraphic profile belongs to a separate site response analysis.

The envelope demand by $V_{S30}$ condition and return period supports the subsequent stages: the site-specific spectra and the elastic design parameters of the structures, the probabilistic estimation of slope displacements, from which the seismic coefficients of the stability verification are derived, and the selection and composition of the time histories of the dynamic analyses.
