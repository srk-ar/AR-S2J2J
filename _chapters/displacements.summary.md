The permanent Newmark displacement quantifies the deformation that an earthquake accumulates in a sliding mass and is the performance measure from which the seismic coefficients of the stability verification are derived. This chapter estimates that displacement $D_n$ probabilistically for the slopes evaluated, combining an ensemble of empirical regression models within a lognormal framework. The rigid-block models—Ambraseys and Menu [-@AmbraseysMenu1988], Jibson [-@Jibson2007], and Saygili and Rathje [-@SaygiliRathje2008]—relate displacement to measures of the base excitation. The flexible-block models—Bray and Travasarou [-@BrayTravasarou2007] and, according to the tectonic regime the project declares, Bray, Macedo, and Travasarou [-@BrayEtAl2018] for subduction or Bray and Macedo [-@BrayMacedo2019] for crustal settings—additionally incorporate the dynamic response of the sliding mass through the fundamental period $T_s$ and the spectral acceleration evaluated at a period proportional to $T_s$; each evaluation applies five formulations. Each model predicts $D_n$ as a function of the yield acceleration $k_y$ and of one or more ground-motion intensity measures—peak ground acceleration, spectral acceleration, or Arias intensity.

The seismic demand for each slope geometry and material class derives from the site-specific uniform hazard spectra; PGA, $S_a(1.3T_s)$, and $S_a(1.5T_s)$ are sampled jointly with the Baker and Jayaram [-@BakerJayaram2008] inter-period correlation model. The overall design ensemble combines the Ambraseys--Menu, Saygili--Rathje, Bray--Travasarou, and the active branch of the Bray--Macedo family with equal weights, while the Jibson formulation is retained as a standalone evaluation; the predictions are propagated with the uncertainty in the site seismic demand to deliver the distribution of $D_n$ by slope and hazard level that underpins the design seismic coefficients.
