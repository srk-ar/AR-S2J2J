Conventional structures require a design spectrum and its elastic parameters derived from the site hazard, established through the site-specific procedures of ASCE/SEI 7-22 [@ASCE722]. Under that standard, the MCER ordinates correspond to the direction of maximum horizontal response with 5% critical damping and are associated with a 1% probability of collapse in 50 years. Each ordinate is determined by that risk target and carries its own return period: this is the difference between uniform risk and uniform hazard.

The elastic design accelerations are obtained as $S_a(T)=\tfrac{2}{3}S_{aM}(T)$ at each period. The $S_{DS}$ and $S_{D1}$ parameters of the site-specific procedure, and their counterparts $S_{MS}=1.5S_{DS}$ and $S_{M1}=1.5S_{D1}$, are determined from those ordinates and reported for each evaluated $V_{S30}$ condition [@ASCE722, secs. 21.3--21.4].

The reported spectra retain the horizontal measure of the ground-motion models. Section 21.2.2 bounds the MCER spectrum from below by the values tabulated in Table 21.2-1 and waives the deterministic calculation where the probabilistic spectrum remains below them at all periods; the 80% floor of Section 21.2.3 is anchored to the USGS Seismic Design Geodatabase. The scope of this chapter is the site-specific probabilistic component and its transformation into design parameters.
