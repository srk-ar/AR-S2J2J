Seismic slope stability assessment through permanent displacements requires the fundamental period of each sliding mass and the dynamic stiffness of the ground that determines it; this chapter estimates both for each material type and slope geometry evaluated. Shear stiffness is characterized with the formulations tabulated by Ishihara [-@Ishihara1996], which express the small-strain shear modulus as a function of void ratio and octahedral effective stress; evaluated over the height of the embankment, the fitted profile defines the base shear modulus $G_o$, the base shear-wave velocity $V_S^o=\sqrt{G_o/\rho}$, with $\rho$ the density of the base layer, and the homogeneity ratio $m_o$ describing the variation of stiffness with depth; the profile also yields the time-averaged shear-wave velocity $V_{S30}$.

The fundamental period $T_s$ is obtained with the truncated inhomogeneous shear-beam model of Dakoulas and Gazetas [-@DakoulasGazetas1985Inhomogeneous], as a function of the height of the geometric scenario, the base shear-wave velocity, and the eigenvalue of the characteristic equation corresponding to the homogeneity ratio $m_o$ and the truncation ratio $\lambda_o$. The parameters are derived separately for each material type and slope geometry, and the mean values of $T_s$ and $V_{S30}$ are the dynamic parameters adopted by the flexible-block models of the displacement analysis.
