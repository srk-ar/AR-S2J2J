The time histories constitute the seismic demand used as input to the dynamic deformation analyses and represent simultaneously its amplitude, its duration, and the distribution of its energy across frequencies. PGA characterizes the peak amplitude; significant duration, the number of cycles near the peak, and the distribution of energy across frequencies contribute to the accumulated deformation of slopes and embankments of cohesionless materials [@Kramer1997; @BrayTravasarou2007].

Candidate motions are screened by magnitude and source-to-site distance ranges tied to the scenarios controlling the hazard, identified from the hazard model and its disaggregation [@NIST2011; @BakerCornell2006]; spectral shape is evaluated by its agreement with the target spectral demand over the period range of the analysis, and at most one record per event is retained. The target spectrum corresponds to the mean of the design demand adopted for the site, and the lower and upper compatibility envelopes to the low and high fractiles of that same demand prescribed in the configuration, with $T_n=0$ as the zero-period acceleration ordinate (PGA). Deterministic component mapping and principal-axis rotation of the two horizontal channels define the canonical H1 component, on which the spectral matching and the verification are performed.

The procedure preserves the frequency content of each record: the adjustment is limited to the amplitude of the retained modes and to one scale coefficient per record. Each history retains the phases, the sequence of cycles, and the non-stationarity of the recorded motion—the properties that contribute to the accumulated deformation of slopes and embankments—and the compatibility of the suite is achieved in the suite-mean spectrum and in the containment of the individual record spectra.

Each record is decomposed by Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) into modes with compact frequency bands [@DragomiretskiyZosso2014]; the first intrinsic mode function (IMF1), the low-frequency component associated with drift and noise, is excluded. The bounded multipliers of the retained modes are adjusted against the compatibility envelope by minimizing a regularized objective, the energy of the reconstructed signal is restored relative to the recorded history, and a positive scale coefficient per record, determined jointly as a bounded quadratic problem, completes the final composition [@VerriKozlowskiInPress]:

$$
Y_i(t)=c_i\,e_i\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}_i}b_i^{(m)}\,x_i^{(m)}(t),
$$

where $x_i^{(m)}(t)$ are the retained modes $\mathcal{M}_i$ of record $i$, $b_i^{(m)}$ the bounded multipliers that adjust its spectral composition, $e_i$ the energy-restoration factor, and $c_i$ the suite-level scale coefficient. The result is a hybrid history: it preserves the temporal structure and the phases of the recorded motion, with its spectral composition adjusted toward the target demand through the amplitude of its modes.

The verification quantifies the relative RMSE between the suite-mean PSA and the target spectrum, the ratio of the mean PGA to the target PGA, the fraction of record–period ordinates contained by the compatibility envelopes, and the range of the scale coefficients. The delivered suite retains the acceleration, velocity, and displacement histories, the intensity measures and the significant durations, the PSA spectra, and the provenance metadata of the source events and stations.
