The seismic design of tailings storage facilities follows the two-level performance philosophy of dam-safety practice: an Operating Basis Earthquake (OBE), which the facility must withstand without operational interruption, and a Safety Evaluation Earthquake (SEE), under which it must not release tailings. ICOLD Bulletin 148 formalizes this framework for embankment structures [@ICOLD2016], and the USACE regulation ER 1110-2-1806 expresses it in terms of the OBE and the Maximum Design Earthquake [@USACE1995].

Three international frameworks implement this philosophy on a consequence basis: the Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management (GISTM), the guidelines of the Canadian Dam Association (CDA), and those of the Australian National Committee on Large Dams (ANCOLD) [@gistm2020; @cda2021; @ancold2019]. The consequence class reflects the potential severity of a failure — population at risk, anticipated loss of life, and environmental and socio-economic impacts — in classes ranging from Low to Extreme, and sets progressively more stringent annual exceedance probabilities (AEPs) for the design earthquakes.

The frameworks differ in structure. GISTM specifies a single earthquake design ground motion for each consequence class and lifecycle phase, with the Maximum Credible Earthquake (MCE) evaluated in parallel and governing whenever it produces the higher demand. CDA adopts the SEE as the design basis, bounded above by the MCE for high-consequence facilities, and permits risk-based departures for lower classes during transitional closure phases. ANCOLD combines a uniform OBE of 1/500 AEP across all consequence classes with an SEE ranging from 1/1,000 to the MCE or the 1/10,000-year event (85th percentile) for the Extreme class.

For a given class, the design earthquake also varies with the lifecycle phase. During construction, partial-height embankments are verified at least at the OBE level, with SEE criteria for high-consequence facilities or high-seismicity regions. Operation concentrates the largest stored volumes and the highest risk profile, and shows the greatest differentiation by class: operational AEPs range from 1/200 (GISTM, Low class) or 1/100 (CDA) to 1/10,000 for Extreme facilities. Post-closure has no uniform criterion: GISTM maintains the class-based criteria through active closure and sets a 1/10,000 AEP for all classes at passive closure; CDA retains category-dependent criteria through closure and passive care, which is not equivalent to abandonment of the facility; ANCOLD adopts the SEE of the corresponding category and requires it to be reassessed at closure when the hazard, the condition of the dam, or its consequences change.

This chapter establishes the probabilistic component of those criteria: for each standard, consequence class, and stage, the design earthquake (DEQ) tables link the prescribed return period to the available uniform hazard spectrum (UHS) and report the mean and the 84th percentile. The MCE verification that the frameworks require in parallel is carried out with the deterministic assessment of this report and compared separately where the standard requires it. The correspondence between the stages compared and the designations of each framework — OBE, SEE, or their post-closure levels — is interpreted by standard and consequence class. The design earthquakes so defined constitute the demand for the stability verifications.
