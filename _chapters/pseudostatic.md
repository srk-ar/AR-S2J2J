The seismic verification of slopes rests on a hierarchy of methods consolidated by international practice: pseudo-static limit-equilibrium analysis as the screening tool, the Newmark sliding-block method as the simplified dynamic verification of performance, and full time-domain dynamic analysis for the highest-consequence cases. ICOLD Bulletin 148 ranks the three tiers explicitly and endorses the Newmark approach as the recommended simplified dynamic method at the Safety Evaluation Earthquake level for embankment structures [@ICOLD2016; @FEMA2005]. Mining regulatory practice concurs: pseudo-static analysis serves as a screening-level tool, with deformation-based verification required for final design in moderate-to-high seismicity regions [@UBCThesis].

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers manuals EM 1110-2-1902 and EM 1110-2-2100 endorse pseudo-static limit-equilibrium analysis among the acceptable methods and address the Newmark approach for estimating permanent seismic deformation. Neither manual prescribes a universal coefficient: both direct the selection of $k_h$ to site-specific seismic hazard and acceptable performance criteria, establishing the principle that the coefficient must reflect project-specific conditions [@USACE2003; @USACE2005].

NCHRP Report 611 codifies displacement-based seismic design for transportation infrastructure: it recommends simplified Newmark displacement correlations for slope performance assessment and a horizontal pseudo-static coefficient of approximately one half of the site-adjusted PGA for limit-equilibrium analyses targeting a minimum factor of safety of 1.1, with indicative performance thresholds of about 100 mm for serviceable conditions and about 300 mm as potentially unacceptable [@AndersonEtAl2008].

Eurocode 8 (EN 1998-5) ties the pseudo-static horizontal coefficient to the design peak ground acceleration and the consequence class of the slope, and permits Newmark displacement calculations as the alternative verification where the critical acceleration substantially exceeds the design PGA [@CEN2004]. Code-based recommendations generally restrict the pseudo-static method to coefficients below approximately 0.3, beyond which dynamic amplification effects become non-negligible.

Taken together, these frameworks support the performance-based definition of the seismic coefficients adopted in this assessment: $k_{\max}$ and $k_h$ are derived from the admissible permanent displacement through the Newmark displacement analysis of Part III, rather than fixed as a generic fraction of PGA.
