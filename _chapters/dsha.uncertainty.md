### Uncertainty Model {#sec-scenario_uncertainty}

The scenario prediction of [@eq-scenario-mixture] combines three distinct sources of uncertainty, and the analysis assigns each one its own representation. Epistemic rupture uncertainty reflects the limited knowledge of the geometry of the postulated rupture: hypocentral depth, dip, rupture style, and plane orientation are constrained only within credible ranges. It is represented by the set of rupture realizations that samples those ranges uniformly: when the available knowledge constrains a parameter only through its bounds, the uniform distribution is the maximum-entropy representation of that state of knowledge, and every realization enters the analysis as an equally credible outcome of the postulated scenario. Epistemic model uncertainty reflects the coexistence of alternative ground-motion models (GMPE) calibrated on different datasets and functional forms; it is represented by the ground-motion logic tree of the scenario's tectonic regime with equal branch weights, following the same maximum-entropy principle ([@sec-gmm_uncertainty]). Aleatory variability describes the record-to-record dispersion of the ground motion for a fixed rupture and model, represented by the normal law of the logarithmic residual in [@eq-gmpe] with the total standard deviation of [@eq-sigma-decomp].

The component deviation $\sigma_k$ in [@eq-scenario-mixture] is the aleatory standard deviation published by model $g$, evaluated for realization $r$. The epistemic spread of the rupture variants and of the model set enters through the dispersion of the medians $\mu_k$ across components, and the scenario distribution as a whole has no single closed-form standard deviation -- its dispersion is reported through the fractiles of [@eq-scenario-moments]. The spectra predicted by each individual formulation are retained alongside the pooled results, together with the physical parameters of every rupture realization and the source-to-site distance metrics derived from its rupture plane, so the reported dispersion remains traceable to its sources.
